# Mixed provenance

A node's *provenance* is simply where it came from: created by a JSX element, or
registered directly on the store. Nothing marks it — the store treats both
identically — but the React presets do not, and that asymmetry is behind a whole
family of bugs where a drop appears to work and then silently does nothing.

This chapter is what happens when one container holds some of each.

A zone can hold children JSX declared *and* children registered on the store.
The two have different lifetimes, and the preset only manages its own.

A preset renders nothing for a child it did not create. Register a node
imperatively under a declarative `<Zone>` and it occupies a strategy slot but
draws no DOM — which looks exactly like a layout bug. `renderImperative` is the
fix:

```tsx
<Zone id={zoneId} strategyId="strip" renderImperative={(node) => <Pane node={node} />}>
  <Panel id="declared" />
</Zone>
```

Now the imperative children render in the box the strategy placed them in, and
the hit-test harvests them like any other child.

## The trap this exists for

**A pane a preset declares in JSX cannot be re-parented by a drop.** JSX owns
that node's lifetime and a preset can only host a node it created. The store
move commits, but the pane keeps rendering in the zone that declared it, with no
rect — it does not appear at the destination at all.

So where a drop may stack, split or move panes between zones, register the panes
on the store and render them through `renderImperative`. Declaring them is what
breaks.
