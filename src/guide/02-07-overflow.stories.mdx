# When panes don't fit

A strip whose panes ask for more than the container has resolves it three ways,
set by `overflowMode` on the container's config.

**`'squeeze'`** (default) scales panes down until their floors bind, then reports
what is left as `LayoutResult.overflow`.

**`'scroll'`** lays out at the extent the panes asked for and reports the whole
excess. `<Container>` and the presets size their box to `viewport + overflow`,
so all you supply is a wrapper that scrolls:

```tsx
<div className="dock-scroll">          {/* overflow-y: auto */}
  <Zone strategyId="strip" config={{ axis: 'y', overflowMode: 'scroll' }}>
```

**`'unplaced'`** places what fits at full extent and routes the rest to
`unplaced` — the same place `maxItems` sends items over its cap, so the two
compose. When even the first pane does not fit it is placed anyway, clamped, so
an overflowing dock never renders empty.

## Why `'scroll'` exists

Content-sized panes. A measurement is a stated size, so under `'squeeze'` a pane
with no `hints.minSize` has nothing to floor it: it shrinks below what it
measured and `overflow` stays *absent*, because nothing bound. Under `'scroll'`
each pane holds at its measurement.

## Grid differs in what triggers it

A grid derives cells from the container, so it can always divide the space and
only overflows once an item states a `minSize` floor. Under `'unplaced'` a
container too narrow for the floors reports width overflow too — dropping rows
cannot widen a cell.

`overflow` is per axis and absent when content fits, so a consumer driving its
own policy can read it and ignore all three modes.

`unplaced` is not an error. Render the items in a tray, or hide them — the
strategy is telling you it declined to place them.
