# What it can do

Three gestures that are most of the reason to reach for a window manager rather
than write one. All three are operable here.

## Drop onto a pane to make a tab stack

A drop in the middle of a pane wraps both in a tabbed container that inherits
the target's slot. Drag the last tab out and the stack dissolves again.

<StackOnDrop />

## Overshoot a seam to absorb a pane

Push a seam past its neighbour's floor and keep pushing: the gesture arms, and
releasing closes that pane. One transaction, so it is one undo step.

<JoinOnOvershoot />

## Drop on an edge to split a slot

A drop near a pane's cross-axis edge turns its slot into a two-pane strip. The
preview lays out the destination as it will actually be, so what you aim at is
what you get.

<SplitOnDrop />

None of these is a special case in the core. Each is a store mutation over the
same node tree, which is why they compose and why undo, snapshot and locks
apply to all of them without knowing what they are.
