# Affordances and seams

An *affordance* is an interactive region a strategy hands back along with its
placements — a resize edge, a gutter between two panes, a click target. The
strategy says where it is and what it adjusts; the host draws it and binds the
pointer. A *seam* is the everyday word for the one between two panes, and
`stripStrategy` calls it a gutter.

That indirection is why resize works on a canvas as readily as in the DOM, and
why this section needs less setup than you would expect.

**Resize costs no new provider.** A `Provider` and a
`StrategyRegistryProvider` is the whole stack — seams bind their own pointer
handlers and read nothing from the drag context. This is the cheapest
interactivity in the library, so check whether it is all you need before reaching for
[§5](/?story=guide--05-drag-and-drop--5-01-wiring-a-drag).

An *affordance* is an interactive region a strategy emits alongside its
placements: a resize edge, a gutter, a click target. The strategy describes it
in layout coordinates; the host renders and binds it.

```tsx
<Zone strategyId="strip" config={{ axis: 'x' }} affordances />
```

<HorizontalStrip />

Strip emits `resize-x-<childId>` after every non-last child. Dragging one
writes an explicit pixel size straight to `membership.placement.size`, which
round-trips through snapshot as an ordinary node field — no separate strategy
state to persist.

The default 4px gutter ships with an 8px hit area (`affordanceHitPad`).

## Constraints are honored

`hints.minSize` is a pixel floor a gutter drag cannot push past, and
`hints.maxSize` a ceiling — on initial layout, explicit sizing, and drags alike.

## Keyboard

Each gutter renders as `role="separator"` with `aria-orientation` and
`aria-valuenow` / `valuemin` / `valuemax`, named after the panes it moves. Arrow
keys along its orientation step by `affordanceKeyStep` (8px); `Home`/`End` jump
to the bounds. Perpendicular arrows are left alone so pane navigation still
works while a gutter holds focus.

Every gutter is a tab stop — the WAI-ARIA window-splitter pattern — which gets
tiring in a dock of many panes. `affordanceTabStops={false}` keeps the ARIA and
drops the stops.

All three are live below: widen the pad and the seam catches the pointer further
out than the line it draws, raise the step and one arrow press moves further,
untick the stops and `Tab` walks past the seams.
