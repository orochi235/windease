# The parallel-zones trap

Two sibling zones, panes dragged between them. This is the canonical
multi-container setup, and the one where provenance bites.

<DragBetween />

## What goes wrong

A drop between zones is a `moveNode` — it rewrites `childOrder` in both. If
either zone **declared** its panes in JSX, reconciliation reverts that on the
next render, and the pane snaps back.

Worse, it does not snap back *visibly* as an error: the store move commits, the
declaring preset keeps rendering the pane in its original zone, and the
destination shows nothing. It reads as a drop that silently failed.

## The fix

Register the panes on the store, render them through `renderImperative` or a
`<Container>` with chrome, and let the store own order:

```tsx
<Zone id={zoneId} strategyId="strip" acceptsDrops renderImperative={renderPane} />
```

Or keep JSX declaring *which* panes exist and hand arrangement to the user with
`preserveStoreOrder`
([3.4](/?story=guide--03-building-the-tree--3-04-who-owns-child-order)).

## Innermost wins

Nested drop targets resolve by DOM depth — `DragController` walks
`parentElement` and the deepest match takes the drop. A zone inside a zone
therefore takes precedence over its parent, which is what you want and worth
knowing when a drop lands somewhere unexpected.

Both zones here also demonstrate hover verdicts: the target carries
`data-drop-target`, and `data-drop-rejected` when it would refuse.
